From the misty hills of Uji

Uji tea fields, Kyoto Prefecture

For over 800 years, the finest matcha has been cultivated in the rolling hills of Uji, where morning mist and volcanic soil create perfect growing conditions.

Three weeks before harvest, the tea plants are shaded under traditional canopies called tana, forcing the leaves to produce more chlorophyll and L-theanine — the amino acid responsible for matcha's uniquely rich umami flavor and calming energy.

Only the youngest, most tender leaves are hand-picked, steamed to preserve their vibrant color, then slowly stone-ground in granite mills — producing just 40 grams per hour of the emerald powder known as matcha.

02

The sacred ritual of tea

一

Sift

Pass 2g of matcha through a fine mesh sieve into your chawan (tea bowl) to break up clumps and ensure a silky smooth texture.

二

Pour

Add 70ml of water heated to 80°C (176°F). The precise temperature preserves matcha's delicate amino acids and prevents bitterness.

三

Whisk

Using a chasen (bamboo whisk), whisk vigorously in a W-motion for 15 seconds until a fine, jade-green froth appears on the surface.

四

Savor

Hold the chawan with both hands. Take a moment of stillness. Then drink in three slow sips — feeling the warmth, the umami, the calm.

"In the taste of a single cup of tea, you will eventually discover the truth of all ten thousand forms in the universe."

— Attributed to Sen no Rikyū, 16th century tea master
